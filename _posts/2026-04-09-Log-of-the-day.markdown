I woke up at 1:30 a.m. today because it was the quarter-final match between FC Barcelona and Atlético Madrid. When I checked, I had already missed the first half—Barcelona were down by one goal and had a red card. Frustrated, I decided to go back to sleep, but I couldn’t. I also didn’t feel like watching the match, so I ended up scrolling through social media until 4:30 a.m. Eventually, I found out we lost by two goals—not the best way to start the day.

Had about 2 hours left before I needed to get up for work. One hour passed just lying there. Didn’t want to go at all. Just wanted to rest. So I messaged my HOD saying someone in my family died — fucking lie (Praise to the lord). Then called the driver and told him I’m not coming.I went back to sleep and woke up again around 7:30 a.m., then had breakfast.

After that, I thought about what to do with the day and decided to try creating a website, inspired by what Yash had done. I spent the entire day learning how to build a website locally using Jekyll and how to make it live using GitHub. At the beginning, it felt quite difficult—I watched a few YouTube videos, but most of it went over my head. Later, I found a really good blog by Programming Historian. That actually made things click.
Overall — rough start, but ended up learning something useful.


(Life until today)

The past week felt like a reflection of my whole year. I’ve been lazy for a long time, and it’s starting to show in my work. But this last week hit differently—I’ve lost confidence. I feel demotivated. Like a lazy bastard who’s just drifting. Spent hours watching reels, trying to feel motivated. Came across Richard Feynman — and something stuck with me. He said anyone can think the way he thinks, if they’re willing to put in the effort, devote time, and truly work hard. There are no “magic people.”

Maybe it’s finally time for me to change.

I don’t want to be a loser anymore. No more laziness. Time to grind. It’s going to be hard, I know that. But I’m ready to put in the effort. God, nature, and all the great minds I look up to—watch over me. I’m willing to work.
 
From tomorrow, I want to become a new person.

Like Hermann Hesse wrote in Siddhartha: to think, to fast, and to wait.

This will be my motto.

“I can think. I can wait. I can fast.”

This time, I don’t just want to say it—I want to live it.
